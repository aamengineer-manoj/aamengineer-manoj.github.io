My story

From a service company to PayPal.

It took more than 20 rejections, tough interviews and a lot of practice. This is how it happened.

  • 2014Accenture
  • 2017Deloitte
  • 2019Hitachi Vantara
  • 2021PayPal
  • 2026MTech, BITS Pilani
01 / where I started

A fresher in a service company.

I joined Accenture in 2014. My first years went into telecom systems for German clients, writing XQuery and Java. Then I moved to Deloitte and built software for a US state government.

I did good work and I was recognised for it. But in a service company the client owns the product. I built what the spec asked for, and I rarely saw what happened to it after release.

02 / the wish

I wanted to own something.

I wanted to own a feature from idea to production and watch real users depend on it. That meant product companies, and almost everyone told me it was hard to get in from service work.

03 / the rejections

Rejected. More than 20 times.

More than 20 rejections. That is how many times I heard no before I heard yes. The interviews were tough and they went deeper than I expected. Interviewers asked follow-up questions about my own projects, about decisions I had never really questioned.

Each rejection hurt. Each one also showed me exactly what was missing.

04 / what changed

I started practising out loud.

Reading was not enough. I practised mock interviews, again and again, until explaining my own work felt natural. Every mock exposed another gap. I fixed it and tried again. I kept trying.

In 2019 I joined Hitachi Vantara, where I owned features end to end on a cloud product. That gave me real stories to tell, with decisions and trade-offs instead of tasks.

05 / the night

31 August 2021, 2 AM.

The offer confirmation arrived in the middle of the night. I did not shout and I did not call anyone. I sat in the silence and felt stillness. Years of late nights, rejections and doubt went quiet. It had been worth it.

The next day, I joined PayPal.

06 / since then

What came after.

At PayPal I work in the Identity team. I shipped address verification and cut name change verification time by 80%. I also completed an MTech in AI and ML at BITS Pilani while working full time.

Today I have taken 500+ technical interviews myself, and the pattern is always the same. The people who get through are not always the smartest. They are the ones who practised and kept going after every no.

07 / what I learned

Three things I would tell my younger self.

  • A rejection is feedback you have not used yet. Read it, fix the gap, try again.
  • Practising out loud beats reading. Interviews are spoken, so practise them that way.
  • Explain decisions, not tasks. Say why you chose something and what you gave up.
